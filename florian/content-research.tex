% ========================== RESEARCH INTERESTS ============================
\section{Research Interests}

{\bodysize
Interpretability of language models, and what can legitimately be inferred from probing their internal representations. Two strands: whether pragmatic and cultural distinctions are genuinely encoded in small bilingual models or reconstructed from surface cues, and how to separate real signal from confounds when probing --- most recently for detecting benchmark contamination. Underlying both is an interest in evaluation methodology, and in the gap between what a benchmark claims to measure and what it measures.
}

% ========================== RESEARCH OUTPUT ===============================
\section{Research Output}

{\bodysize\textbf{Excess Separability: Nuisance-Controlled Residual-Stream Probing for Benchmark Contamination Detection}\hfill{\smallsize\color{subtle}Aug 2026}}\par
\vspace{1pt}
{\bodysize\color{subtle}\textit{Preprint --- not peer reviewed} \quad\href{https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.12652}{\smallsize arXiv:2608.12652}}\par
\vspace{2pt}
\begin{bullets}
    \item Shows that naive linear probing of transformer activations fails as a contamination test: false positive rates range from 0.03 to 0.99 under the null depending on control-set size.
    \item Proposes a zero-sum contrast on the depth profile of probe accuracy, recentred on a level-matched placebo baseline. All four well-matched Pile arms return null, and the protocol abstains on temporal splits rather than reporting an unreliable verdict.
\end{bullets}

\vspace{4pt}
{\bodysize\textbf{Interpretable Cross-Lingual Alignment in Small Language Models: Probing Cultural and Pragmatic Reasoning in Japanese--English Bilingual LLMs}\hfill{\smallsize\color{subtle}Aug 2026}}\par
\vspace{1pt}
{\bodysize\color{subtle}\textit{Preprint --- not peer reviewed} \quad\href{https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.14896}{\smallsize arXiv:2608.14896}}\par
\vspace{2pt}
\begin{bullets}
    \item Introduces J-PragEval-v0, a benchmark for Japanese pragmatic phenomena --- honorifics, in-group / out-group reference, context-dependent politeness, and zero anaphora --- evaluated on TinySwallow-1.5B with linear probes and teacher forcing.
    \item Honorific register is cleanly linearly decodable from the residual stream (0.96 balanced accuracy at layer 15, 93\% flip rate); implicit subjects and in-group reference are not (0.48 and 0.38) yet still shift generation at 0.77 and 0.79 flip rates.
    \item Proposes Pragmatic Representation Steering, an inference-time method for editing residual-stream activations.
\end{bullets}
